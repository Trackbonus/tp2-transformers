Dawn of Victory

Rhapsody of Fire

View all reviews for Rhapsody of Fire - Dawn of Victory

Bombastic and epic - 85%

The Clansman 95, July 31st, 2018

Rhapsody of Fire need no introduction. They are definitely the most famous and renowned metal band from Italy, and, although they like to coin strange terms to define their musical output, like "hollywood metal" or "cinematic metal", their style is amenable to the symphonic/neoclassical power metal genre. Well-known for their incredibly epic and bombastic musical approach, fusing metal with classical, baroque and sometimes folk music, for their fantasy-based lyrics and the huge employment of choirs and orchestras, this six-piece musical ensemble, hailing from Trieste, has gathered a wide legion of fans throughout the years.

"Dawn of Victory" is their third major release, and marks an important transition in the evolution of the band's musical style. With this album, while retaining the choral and orchestral elements they are famous for, Rhapsody chose to give more relevance to the lead guitar parts, and to the keyboard leads too: the result is an album which is less symphonic and more aggressive, closer to the traditional expression of heavy/power metal (at least, if we compare it to the band's two previous releases). Musically speaking, however, the key ingredients of Rhapsody's "hollywood metal" recipe are still there, such as the heavy use of the minor harmonic scale, the overwhelming presence of sweeps in almost every solo, and the aforementioned employment of choral and orchestral arrangements.

As some of you may know, great part of Rhapsody's discography consists of concept albums, part of two great fantasy sagas, the first being the "Emerald Sword Saga"; this album narrates the events occurring during the third part of the aforementioned story, and it's here that we have the real turning point of the whole plot. The lyrics become more and more dramatic as the album flows, as the hero (Warrior of Ice) and his friends, after saving the city of Ancelot from the hordes of the Black King Akron, are eventually captured, tortured and killed, save for the protagonist himself.

The drumming in this album is particularly thunderous and mighty, providing some interesting fills; the guitar riffs are definitely interesting, relying mainly on tremolo picking and power chords; the guitar solos are really technical, almost virtuoso-like, yet extremely melodic, centered around sweep picking, which is definitely guitarist's Luca Turilli favourite technique; the keyboard is more prominent than in previous albums, as it provides a lot of amazing, furious lightspeed solos, confirming once again Alex Staropoli as one of the best keyboard players in the metal scene; the bass is clearly audible and does have his time to shine, providing some nice solos too. Fabio Lione's semi-operatic vocals are as strong as ever, and fit the music perfectly; his vibrato technique and his ability to hit even the higher notes in a register that is so close to the chest voice are nothing short of amazing. Really one of the best power metal singers out there.

The songs display variety and originality, resulting fresh yet cohesive as a whole; we have fast-paced, aggressive tracks, like the title track "Dawn of Victory", "Dargor, Shadowlord of the Black Mountain", or the anthemic lead single "Holy Thunderforce" (and believe me when I say the guitar riff in this song, the solos and the chorus are nothing short of amazing); slower and softer tracks like "The Bloody Rage of the Titans" or "The Village of Dwarves" (which is characterized by a wonderful folk-like melody played by the keyboards); we have even a short instrumental and a nine-minute epic, called "The Mighty Ride of the Firelord", which brings the bombast at the maximum level before gloriously closing the album. Each song is a display of high-level musicianship, and the riffs, but especially the vocal melodies, will remain stuck in your head for a long time, being extremely catchy and well written.

Although the perfect balance between the symphonic and the more aggressive, guitar-centered style of Rhapsody will be fully reached in their fifth full-length album, "Power of the Dragonflame" (surely the best release of their career), "Dawn of Victory" still stands as one of the strongest works by the most loved and bombastic european symphonic power metal band. I can assure you, once you'll have finished listening to this album, you'll immediately feel the urge to grab a sword, put on an armour and go slaying some demons for the sake of love and cosmic justice. Recommended to any power metal aficionado.
